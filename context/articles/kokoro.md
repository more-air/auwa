## WHY THIS ARTICLE EXISTS

Auwa uses Kokoro as its own name for the life force that resides in all things. It is in the
brand philosophy, the About page description, `llms.txt`, and the welcome email. **And there is no
page for it.** That is the largest single gap in the site's SEO picture: Auwa claims a word it has
never written about.

**Read the competitive reality before planning this.** Beth Kempton — a bestselling author with two
degrees in Japanese, translated into 24 languages, covered by TIME and Vogue — has published
**KOKORO: Japanese Wisdom for a Life Well Lived**, following *Wabi Sabi*. The term is getting
harder, not easier, and her book, its publisher pages, Amazon and Goodreads will hold the top of
that search for years.

**So judge this piece on brand depth, not on rank.** Write it because Auwa cannot keep using the
word without a page behind it, and because it anchors the philosophy cluster. Expect modest
traffic. Full reasoning in `context/pillar/photography.md` → THE PHILOSOPHY CLUSTER.

**Auwa's actual edge is authorship, not expertise.** Kempton is a British writer who studied
Japanese. Rieko is Japanese and grew up inside these ideas in Kansai. **Do not try to out-explain
someone with two degrees in the subject.** Write from the inside: what the word does in a Japanese
household, what it means that English needs four words for it, how it is used in ordinary speech.
An explainer loses. A first-hand account does not.

**Sequencing note.** The cluster is better built from the edges inward: publish `kuyo` and one or
two other specific concepts (mono no aware, ma, mottainai, yugen) first, interlink them, and let
Kokoro sit at the centre once there is something for it to anchor. **Consider publishing this
third or fourth, not first.**

## THE SUBJECT

Kokoro (心) holds as one undivided thing what English splits into heart, mind, soul and spirit.
There is no clean translation, which is why Auwa keeps the Japanese word.

Points the piece must get right, because the site has been careful about them elsewhere:

- **The belief is Japanese and ancient; the naming is Auwa's own.** The tradition itself calls the
  life force *kami* (Yaoyorozu no Kami). Auwa calls it Kokoro. Attributing the word Kokoro to the
  ancient tradition is the error the site corrected in August 2026 — do not reintroduce it. Correct
  form: the Japanese have long believed a life force resides in all things, and in Auwa we call it
  Kokoro.
- **Do not describe any of it as Shinto.** See `llms.txt` and `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Awareness points outward, not inward.** Western wellness centres on the self; Auwa draws on
  Japanese collectivist thinking. Kokoro extends to the maker, the season, the stranger.
- Include **one clean, quotable definition near the top.** This is what wins featured snippets and
  gets cited in AI answers.
