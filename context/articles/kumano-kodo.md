## THE ANGLE

**Do not chase "Kumano Kodo" as the primary term.** Page one is JNTO, Wikipedia and tour
operators, and the intent behind it is trip planning, not reading. Auwa cannot win it and would not
want the traffic.

**Lead on the walk ending in water.** Tsuboyu is the only UNESCO World Heritage bath on earth you
are permitted to bathe in: a wooden hut over a stream, a stone pool that fits two, thirty minutes
on a numbered ticket, in a village that has been bathing pilgrims for roughly eighteen hundred
years. Competition is thin guide pages and a few personal blogs; nobody has done it as editorial
with real photography, and Auwa has the ticket in shot.

## FACTS CHECKED (safe to use)

- Tsuboyu is the only UNESCO World Heritage listed bath that can be bathed in.
- Small wooden cabin over the stream, stone-lined pool, fits one or two people.
- Thirty minutes per group, numbered ticket bought at the Yunomine public bath.
- The source water is hot enough to boil eggs.
- Yunomine is around 1,800 years old and among the oldest hot springs in Japan.
- Tsuboyu is about 90 minutes on foot from Kumano Hongu Taisha (the Dainichi-goe).
- The Nakahechi is the imperial route, walked by emperors from the 10th century.
- Ōyunohara is the original site of Hongu, moved after the 1889 flood.

The legend on the Tsuboyu building is Oguri Hangan, healed by the water. Worth a line, but check
the story before asserting details.
