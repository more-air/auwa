## THE STORY (all of it verified from Fin's own posts)

Fin DAC is an Irish urban artist, ~96K Instagram followers, known for stencil portraits of Asian
women. He and Tom are old friends from Tom's digital advertising days.

1. **2020.** Tom asks Fin about a portrait commission of Rieko. Fin rarely takes commissions.
2. **The source photographs.** Tom shows him options, including one of **Rieko's mother Mitsuko on
   her wedding day** — in Fin's words, "a beautiful vintage looking photo with washed out colours."
3. **The idea.** Fin decides to use Mitsuko's photograph as the basis, but to show **Rieko wearing
   her mother's wedding dress**. He explicitly checked this was not disrespectful. Both Rieko and
   Mitsuko loved it.
4. **The making.** Fin's stated first "art love" is Japanese woodblock prints — he names Kikugawa
   Eizan, Torii Kiyonaga, Utagawa Kunisada and Kitagawa Utamaro. This was his first real use of
   watercolour, Japanese ink and brush, masking fluid and tea-staining for a vintage look, finished
   with metallic paint and gold leaf "as all the vintage woodblock prints were." He points people
   to the Chester Beatty in Dublin for the best print collection he has seen.
5. **January 2022.** He posts it as his first artwork of 2022. ~1,057 likes.
6. **April 2022.** Tom sends him a photograph of it framed at home in the afternoon light. Fin
   reposts it, and says: *"I don't do commissions very often but this was a great opportunity to
   explore new creative angles… 100% I'm going to do more pieces like this."*
7. **The ending.** As thanks for the inspiration, Fin promised he would make **a second version for
   Mitsuko**. Tom and Rieko delivered it to her at home in Japan in May 2024. Fin posted the
   photographs: *"This makes me very happy."*

**That ending is the article.** A portrait travels from a mother's wedding photograph, through an
Irish artist in London who loves ukiyo-e, onto her daughter, and then back to the mother as a gift.
Two women, one dress, fifty years apart.

**The Auwa coda.** On a later studio visit Fin cut a stencil of the Auwa character and demonstrated
it. Photographs 13 and 14 below are that stencil. **OPEN: Tom to decide whether this belongs in
this article or is held back.** The argument for holding it: the piece above is complete and about
Rieko's family, and Auwa arriving at the end could read as the brand inserting itself into a
personal story. The argument for including it: it is genuinely how Auwa and Fin connect, and it
sets up everything that follows. My recommendation is **a short closing section, understated**, one
photograph, no pitch. Tom to confirm. Tom also wants to ask Fin whether he still has the stencil.
